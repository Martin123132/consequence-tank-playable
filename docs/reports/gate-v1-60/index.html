Executive Summary

  • Timing changes the full-run result. Memory averages -4.3, +5.15, +11.25 deliveries versus Direct for early, middle and late gate changes. Early changes reduce deliveries in 18/20 seeds; late changes increase them in 20/20.
  • Full-run gains hide post-change harm. Memory takes more damage in the first 100 post-change steps in 56/60 comparisons, the same in 2, and less in 2. Mean extra damage is +28.4, +10.7, +23.8 synthetic units for early, middle and late changes.
  • These are separate trade-offs, not a winner score. Movement energy excludes computation and memory cost. The 60 comparisons reuse 20 fixed seeds across three timings; this is not 60 independent seeds or a real-world benchmark.
[1] Fixed protocol and complete simulation outputs

A later change improves full-run totals

At step 800, Memory delivers more and takes less total damage in all 20 seeds. At step 260, it takes more total damage in all 20 and never delivers more. Later changes leave a longer pre-change period: full-run gains alone do not establish better adaptation after the move. [1]

Damage rises even with equal post-change exposure

The exposure is exactly 100 steps, including the change step, at every timing. Memory's mean damage is higher in each case. Mean delivery differences are 0, +0.2, -0.85. This is consistent with stale-route costs, but this report does not isolate an individual internal mechanism as the cause. [1]

Development implication

Keep gate-v1 as the fixed reference. A future, separately versioned experiment could test whether observed-harm feedback or memory decay reduces first-100 damage without losing deliveries. That experiment is not part of this report; these results do not establish that either change would work.

Method and evidence [1]

  1. Seeds 5000-5019 were fixed before this run and reused at changes 260, 520 and 800. Every pair runs to 900. All 60 pairs are included; no failing case was omitted or substituted.
  2. Checkpoints are change-1, change+99 and 900. The first-100 window includes change through change+99. All-post-change windows have 641, 381 and 101 steps respectively, so raw remaining-window totals must not be treated as equal-exposure comparisons.
  3. Every difference is Success memory minus Direct routing within a matched seed/timing pair. Means, medians, ranges and lower/same/higher counts are descriptive. Ranges are observed extrema, not confidence intervals. Repeated seeds are not independent evidence across timings.
  4. Displayed numbers are rounded; raw JSON and CSV retain full precision. In signed counts, absolute differences at or below 0.000001 are treated as equal to avoid floating-point noise.
  5. The frozen gate-v1 source is hash-checked before and after execution. Both policies retain identical initial conditions, per-event costs, task streams and observations; shared success memory is the intended difference. Neither policy uses Oracle planning or knows the future change time.
  6. Gate changes make cells harmful, not impassable. Shelves are visual landmarks. Collisions, queues, realistic charging, real robot physics and computational cost are not modelled. These synthetic quantities are not operational energy bills, monetary savings or safety measurements.
  7. Example selection was fixed before execution: largest full-run delivery difference; largest common-window damage difference; closest absolute delivery difference, then damage, then energy. Ties use ascending seed, then timing. Independent rules can select the same case. They illustrate, rather than represent, the sample.
